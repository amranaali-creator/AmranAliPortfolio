Arca Redesign

Client Furnitubes
Role Project Instigator & Designer
Type Product Redesign

The Arca is a modular bin that allows users to customise the product across over 32 different functional and visual styles. Its principle design focuses on a core structure with changeable parts.

After several years on the market and a new series of on-boardings, I noticed that the bin was very complicated to explain and understand. It was also laborious to process and expensive to produce.

The challenge was to make it simpler and easier to communicate, make and sell — without losing the visual character that had made it successful.

Phase One

Range Review

The first step to understanding this problem was to map the range out. Working closely with the sales and marketing team we were able to identify what actually sold and what to focus on.

Over 76% of sales and enquiries were for a third of the range — primarily the bin with a lid, in both circular and square forms. This gave us a clear direction for where to focus design effort.

The initial review allowed us to refine the range to what worked in the market and aligned it to business and manufacturing goals of efficiency.

Phase Two

Research & Development

A truly modular design

We used the review findings to redevelop and improve the design in light of manufacturing changes — namely the move away from galvanising.

Reviewing the original purpose of the design, which was to offer a truly modular bin, we proposed a new universal chassis that allows components of both the circular and square styles to fit without the need of new fabrications.

The aim was to improve the details without impacting the visuals too much, as the latter were part of the product's current success.

Phase Three

Commercial Review

Using competitor analysis we were able to benchmark this design. Working closely with marketing and sales data, we proposed a new strategy for the product: batch production and stocking.

This would allow the product to be more competitively priced and take advantage of automated manufacturing. My role here was to work on different iterations of the design and gather costs.

This information was presented back to stakeholders to ensure the idea would be feasible before committing to the next phase of development.

Phase Four

Detailing & Prototyping

Using all the learnings from the previous phases we created the design more formally, assisting the marketing team with the communication of the product.

My focus was to ensure the details were collated and executed. Working alongside the engineering team and factory to create a system for drawing, manufacturing and processing the product using the business ERP system.

We then built a prototype to test not only the product design but the methods for processing — ensuring the new manufacturing approach was viable at scale.

The result

A core range of 12 products that offer a simple and functional bin that is truly modular. With ambitions of being stockable for faster lead times and easier customisation, this refined iteration secured more specifications and sales for the business.
